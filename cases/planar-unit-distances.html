Genericity can fail after enlarging the coefficient field

Section 8.2 · Lemma 8.3

Issue

At the end of Section 8.2, the paper asserts that a point generic over a coefficient field stays generic after adjoining other grid points. The grid lemma guarantees joint independence over the base field B₀, but not over an arbitrary larger field.

Counterexample

Let p and r be distinct grid vertices, with p on a curve or plane V over B₀ of dimension d ∈ {1, 2}. The selected coordinates are jointly algebraically independent over B₀; each vertex’s remaining coordinates are algebraic over B₀ and its own selected coordinates.

Choose selected coordinates a of p and b of r, and set B = B₀(a + b). Since b is transcendental over B₀(p), the point p remains generic over B, with transcendence degree d. But adjoining r gives:

a = (a + b) − b ∈ B(r)
trdeg over B(r) of B(r, p) ≤ d − 1

One of p’s independent coordinates is now in the coefficient field. Thus p is no longer generic on V over B(r). The evaluation criterion that explicitly assumes genericity is unaffected.

Proposed repair by OSVerify

Restrict automatic preservation to adjoining other grid points to B₀. Their selected coordinates are jointly independent of the point’s selected coordinates, and adjoining their remaining curve coordinates is algebraic, so the point remains generic. For an arbitrary larger coefficient field, retain the explicit genericity hypothesis. The application over K_0 already establishes that hypothesis separately at lines 3600–3612; the later anchor applications use independence over B₀ and algebraic extensions. This correction resolves the identified overstatement without changing those arguments.
