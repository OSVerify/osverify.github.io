Major · Issue 01Link ↗

The bad-density chains lemma is missing an endpoint hypothesis

Section 8.5 · Lemma 8.6

Issue & evidence

The bad-density chains lemma restricts its entire nesting display to i<N, leaving P_{N,h}⊆C_{N,h} unstated. Its proof nevertheless uses this inclusion in the log-mismatch indicator bound: without it, c_{N,h}−p_{N,h−Δ} can be negative while the indicator equals one. This identifies a defect in the proof under the written hypotheses, not a counterexample to the final numerical conclusion. The subsequent PRE/CURRENT graph application supplies the missing inclusion.

Proposed repair by OSVerify

Separate the nesting hypothesis into P_{i,h}⊆C_{i,h} for every 1≤i≤N and C_{i,h}⊆P_{i+1,h} for i<N. Together with monotonicity in height, these justify the nonnegative log differences and the temporal nesting of the C sets used in the proof. PRE refinement of CURRENT supplies the first condition at every event, and the linked-event inclusion supplies the second. This endpoint correction requires no change to the chain-length bound used in the linear-side energy argument.

Locations in the paper

Section 8.5; Lemma 8.6; Proof of Lemma 8.6; Section 8.4; Equation (22); Section 8.6; Proof of Proposition 8.9.

Minor · Issue 02Link ↗

The predictor identity omits a conditional-expectation term

Section 5.2 · Lemma 5.3 · Equation (7)

Issue & evidence

The predictor identity at lines 2708–2715 omits a term for the general augmented inputs admitted in this section. Orthogonality gives μ_h=εa_{h,p}−√ε E_h[(F_a)_p], so its increment also includes −√ε(E_{h+1}−E_h)[(F_a)_p]. For example, F_a=Φ_p has zero residual and predictor coefficient one, contradicting μ_h=εa_{h,p}. The stated identity is valid for scalar embeddings, and this counterexample does not disprove estimate (7).

Proposed repair by OSVerify

Set f_p=(F_a)_p and use μ_h=√ε[(P_hF_a)_p−E_hf_p]. The projection increments and conditional-expectation increments each have a sum of squared L²(J) norms at most ||F_a||²₂. Consequently (∑_h||μ_{h+1}−μ_h||²₂)^(1/2)≤2√ε||F_a||₂ and ||μ_H||₂≤2√ε||F_a||₂. In the existing proof, these bounds retain the 2^(−K_0), K_0√ε, and √ε contributions with adjusted absolute constants. Thus this correction preserves the parameter dependence needed for (7) and its high-truncation application; for scalar embeddings the added term vanishes.

Section 3.2; Lemma 3.2; Section 5.1; Section 5.2; Lemma 5.3; Equation (7); Proof of Lemma 5.3; Section 5.4; Proof of Proposition 5.6.

Minor · Issue 03Link ↗

The first-failing-role convention uses the wrong order

Section 9.1 · Section 9.2

Issue & evidence

Lines 6789–6793 contain a local convention mismatch. Assignment to the first failing role requires each role to succeed at trials assigned to later roles. Requiring success at trials assigned to earlier roles instead describes assignment to the last failing role. Both conventions partition failed trials, so this mismatch does not itself invalidate the subsequent summed estimates.

Proposed repair by OSVerify

Keep the first-failing-role convention and replace “the earlier roles' required positions” with “the trials assigned to later roles.” Explicitly, if a(t) is the assigned role, role j must fail when a(t)=j and succeed when a(t)>j, with no condition when a(t)<j. Retain success in every core role at the final height. These corrected masks still select whole minimal-component core subtotals by predicate coherence. For each fixed assignment, the assigned failure counts n_j remain unchanged, and the upper bounds at lines 7052–7071 still follow by discarding the additional success conditions.

Locations in the paper

Section 9.1; Section 9.2.
