Major · Issue 01Link ↗

The absolute pairing fails to preserve direct sums

Section 4.3 · Proposition 4.3 · Equation (4.3)

Issue

The absolute pairing (4.3) is asserted to be continuous for the stated parameter class, which includes non-quasi-compact spaces.

Counterexample

Take S to be a countable disjoint union of points with constant marking, H = SL₂, X = ℙ¹, and s = 0. The zero-label stratum is 𝔸¹; the normalized pairing of opposite exponential objects is ℂ.

Let W carry the positive object on every component, and Vₙ the negative object only on component n. All lie in the zero-pole bound. Ordinary global sections give:

B(W, ⨁ₙ Vₙ) = ∏ₙ ℂ
⨁ₙ B(W, Vₙ) = ⨁ₙ ℂ

The canonical comparison from the direct sum to the product is not an isomorphism. Increasing pole bounds does not remove the failure. This tests the stated generality at s = 0; it is not a counterexample at irrational level, and does not challenge the relative pairing or the pairing on Xᴵ.

Correction requested by OSVerify

For the stated non-quasi-compact parameter generality, distinguish ordinary absolute de Rham sections from a continuous categorical evaluation and justify the construction supporting the asserted duality. Check agreement with (4.3) on quasi-compact parameters and compatibility with the claimed closed-image transposition identities. In the disjoint-union example, continuity requires the direct sum of the componentwise evaluations; that observation alone does not construct the evaluation for general S. The current extension in pole bounds does not supply this parameter-direction argument.

Minor · Issue 02Link ↗

Constant fibres do not imply a global scalar identification

Section 6.1 · Proof of Proposition 6.1

Issue

The global scalar-identity assertion in the finite-averaging argument is too strong: constant fibre cohomology does not eliminate a torsor’s derived extension data.

Counterexample

After the stated cancellations, the diagonal coefficient is E = p*ωH×N, where p(h′, n) = h′B⁻, up to fixed normalization shifts. For H = SL₂, the constant-sheaf counterpart is Rπ*ℂSL₂ on ℙ¹ for π : SL₂ → ℙ¹. Here B⁻ is the opposite Borel subgroup and N the unipotent subgroup in the averaging construction.

With complex coefficients and dimensions summed over all degrees, a constant derived coefficient with fibre H*(B⁻) would give total dimension four. The actual pushforward instead has the cohomology of SL₂, of total dimension two:

dim H*(ℙ¹) · dim H*(B⁻) = 2 · 2 = 4
dim H*(SL₂) = 2

The claimed global scalar identification fails. The report proposes a repair of conservativity using the actual coefficient and descent.

Proposed repair by OSVerify

Keep E=p*ωH×N, with p(h',n)=h'B⁻. Trivialize H→H/B⁻ on translates of the big cell. On each covering open U, the coefficient becomes ω_U⊗V, where V=Γ_dR(B⁻×N,ω) is a nonzero finite complex. Over ℂ, V splits into shifted finite-dimensional vector spaces, so tensoring with V detects zero for arbitrary input complexes. Descent therefore makes tensoring with E conservative globally. The same calculation on products with spectator charts preserves spectators. Together with the preceding diagonal-support calculation, this proves conservativity of the composite and hence of finite averaging, supplying the conservativity needed downstream without asserting a global splitting.

Minor · Issue 03Link ↗

Three references point to the wrong equation

Section 10.5 · Proof of Proposition 10.6

Issue & evidence

The references to (4.1) at lines 7026, 7134, and 7139 are inaccurate: that equation displays finite-loop averaging, whereas these passages refer to the zero-defect phase, the parameter map q_D, and the stratum projector. The intended definitions and projector are present elsewhere in the submission, so this is a nonblocking cross-reference defect.

Correction requested by OSVerify

Correct the reference at line 7026 to the definition of psi_D at lines 1760–1764, specialized to D=0. At line 7134, refer to the definition of q_D at lines 1725–1734 and the formula (4.2). At line 7139, replace (4.1) with (4.2). Add labels to the definitions if needed; the associated shift calculation is already given at lines 1856–1862.
