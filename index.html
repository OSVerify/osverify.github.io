One-Sided Verification Under Limited Expert Review

Fan Nie*, Haotian Ye*, Shurui Liu, Zihao Wang, Stefano Ermon, James Zou

Stanford University

*Equal contribution

Paper coming soon Code coming soon Blog BibTeX

OSVerify is a verifier for settings where AI proposes candidate solutions faster than human experts can check them. It is one-sided: it rejects as many invalid candidates as possible while keeping the rate of wrongly rejected valid solutions under a fixed budget, so expert time goes to the candidates most likely to be correct. Rather than judging a whole solution at once, OSVerify decouples error proposal from error validation. A proposer searches the solution at every level of detail for errors that could invalidate it, and a validator rejects only on errors it confirms as substantial. On mathematical benchmarks it cuts the invalid candidates experts must review by 46% relative to voting, or 57% with an RL-trained validator, and on research papers it finds errors that authors and experts confirm.

01The problem

Valid solutions to hard problems are rare, so discarding one is costly. At the same time, every invalid candidate that reaches an expert costs review time. We therefore treat verification as a constrained objective rather than a single accuracy number:

maximize TNR(V) subject to FNR(V) ≤ τ TNR: fraction of invalid solutions the verifier rejects. FNR: fraction of valid solutions it wrongly rejects. Under a fixed FNR budget τ, every gain in TNR removes invalid candidates from the expert's queue.

02How it works

Checking a specific, stated error is easier than finding one. With the same model, AUROC rises from 0.68 to 0.90 when it judges a proposed error instead of the whole solution. OSVerify builds on this in three steps.

Propose

Split the solution recursively and search every part, from the whole argument down to single claims, for errors that could invalidate it.

Validate

Check each proposed error on its own: does it hold, and does it invalidate the solution?

  • Not-Real
  • Minor
  • Substantial

Decide

Reject if any error is confirmed Substantial, with a threshold set to meet the FNR budget. Otherwise, pass the solution to an expert.

Because the validator always sees the same input, a problem, a solution and one proposed error, it can be post-trained with reinforcement learning across problems.

03Evidence

Refereeing research papers

In the paper, OSVerify found 45 issues in a paper published in the Annals of Mathematics and 88 in a recent preprint, compared with 11 and 3 from direct review with the same model. The authors confirmed the errors and revised their papers.

We now also run OSVerify as a referee on new mathematics preprints from OpenAI. Across 10 papers, it has flagged 3 major and 14 minor issues, each verified by human experts. The papers with major issues:

Resolving open conjectures

Used as the verifier for an agentic mathematical problem solver, with no formal proof checker in the loop, OSVerify helped produce proofs of three previously open statements in relative Langlands duality:

  1. flatness of the loop-rotated Plancherel algebra PLX,ħ over k[ħ] (Ben-Zvi–Sakellaridis–Venkatesh, Conj. 8.5.2);
  2. commutativity of PLX (Conj. 8.1.8), with (i) and (ii) in the setting of Liu–Wang, Conj. 4.36;
  3. the automorphic commutator relation (Liu–Wang, Conj. 4.45), previously known only under extra cohomological vanishing assumptions and for curves of genus other than one.

Human experts have confirmed the proofs, and the results are being prepared for submission to mathematical journals.
